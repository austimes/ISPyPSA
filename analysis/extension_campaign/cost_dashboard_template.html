The cost of supplying the NEM under demand growth and decarbonisation pressure

Forty-one recursive-dynamic chains — five demand trajectories crossed with ten pressure settings — solved over 2030, 2040, 2050 and 2060 on the full National Electricity Market. The instrument here is mostly the cap: six absolute annual CO2e schedules ending between 0.02 and 0.0005 t/MWh in 2050, with four carbon prices run on two trajectories to calibrate what those caps are worth. This page reads one thing off that grid: what electricity costs to supply, on average and at the margin, as demand and pressure move — and where the pressure outruns the fleet and load is shed instead.

The cost pathway over time

AUD/MWh

Average cost against milestone year at the selected trajectory, one line per pressure setting. 2060 sits on authored demand inputs rather than an AEMO-published scenario year, so read the last step of every pathway as an extension of the campaign's own assumptions, not as an AEMO projection.

The implied price of each cap

AUD/tCO2e · log scale

What each cap was worth in the solve: the shadow price of the annual CO2e constraint, negated, averaged over the trajectories that served their demand at that milestone. This is the number that makes caps and prices comparable — the dashed reference lines are the three calibration prices actually run. A cap whose implied price runs far above them is priced outside the calibrated range. A missing bar means every trajectory at that cap and milestone shed load, so the only shadow prices on offer report the A$10,000/MWh shedding penalty rather than the cost of abatement. The axis is logarithmic, the only one on this page: a cap approaching infeasibility prices abatement three orders of magnitude above a shallow one, and each gridline is a tenfold step.

Marginal cost of demand

AUD/MWh

What the next increment of demand costs: each bar is the mean of the measured trajectory-step arcs at that milestone and pressure, and the ink ticks are the arcs themselves — their spread is the measured step-to-step variation. Every arc is a difference of two solved chains, both carried in marginals.csv. Boundary cells are excluded from every arc, so a pressure setting with few bars is one whose deeper cells shed load.

The projection — (demand, renewable fraction) coordinates

An interim projection into a companion representation's coordinates

A companion representation is parameterised in (demand, renewable fraction) coordinates rather than (demand, pressure setting). The panels below re-plot the same solved cells in those coordinates, so the team can see where these solutions land in that space ahead of a fraction-controlled campaign.

This is a projection of pressure-driven solutions, not a mapping of the fraction space. Coverage is a set of pressure-traced curves per milestone, not a grid, and each plotted fraction is where the cap or price put it, not a target that was hit.

Coverage map — where the solutions sit in fraction space

% of generation

Every solved cell, positioned by delivered energy and realised renewable fraction, coloured by the pressure setting that produced it. Read the vertical spread within a milestone as what the cap ladder buys, and the slope of each line as how much of an extra demand increment that setting meets renewably. Blank regions bound what this projection can say; nothing licenses interpolation across them.

Fraction = renewable share of grid generation in this model's topology, which excludes rooftop PV, and excludes unserved energy from both numerator and denominator. It is not on the same denominator as externally quoted shares, so no numerical comparison against authored anchors until the convention is agreed — which is also why none are overlaid on any panel in this group.

Resource cost against realised fraction

The projection of the cost surface onto the companion representation's cost-of-fraction axis: one curve per demand trajectory, points at each pressure setting. Resource cost is average cost minus the carbon payment, which only the price chains carry. The payment is the instrument that induced the fraction and would not exist under a fraction target, so including it would overstate the cost of achieving a given fraction by the size of the cheque.

Cost basis
Each point is the cheapest system the model found under a cap or a price, not the cheapest way to reach that renewable fraction — a fraction-targeted solve could reach the same fraction at equal or lower resource cost, so these points sit on or above the true cost-of-fraction curve.

Implied fraction-cost slope: Δ cost ÷ Δ realised fraction between adjacent pressure settings at the selected trajectory. These are arcs along the pressure-traced manifold, not a cost-of-fraction curve, and the denominator is small at the top of the range. — marks a suppressed arc: Δ fraction under 2 percentage points, a cost delta inside the ±0.5 AUD/MWh noise floor, a missing cell, or a boundary cell. Fraction convention and on-manifold bias as per the coverage map's footnote and the caveats.

Which axis matters, and how to read it

Which axis moves cost more

AUD/MWh

The full span of each axis, measured on the cells that serve their demand: uncapped to deepest feasible cap, averaged over trajectories, against lightest to heaviest demand trajectory, averaged over pressure settings. These are full spans, not per-unit effects: the cap ladder is geometric and has no natural unit, so normalising it the way a carbon price can be normalised would invent one.

How to read these numbers

The acceptance record behind every figure on this page, and the resolution limit that applies to all of them (stated in full in the caveats).

Differences below the noise floor — about ±0.5 AUD/MWh on average costs, of order ±10 AUD/MWh on an arc marginal — are within solver tolerance and are not read as signal anywhere on this page.
